# The Speaker Playbook — what to say

> Don't be boring. Be unforgettable.

This is the **"what to say" layer** of better-slides. Read it during content work (Phase 1)
*before* any design decision. It governs structure and message; the engine and styles
govern how it lands. A beautiful deck with no thesis is slop — this playbook is the thesis.

The method moves: **strategy → visual → delivery → engagement → impact → mindset.**

---

## 1. Cut it down to one idea

Everything you *could* say → what you *choose* to say → what they'll *remember*.

- Distill the whole talk to **one big idea**.
- **Can't say it in one breath? It's not one idea yet.** Keep cutting.
- Keep the 2–3 strongest points that serve that idea. Drop the rest — they dilute.

> In the deck: state the one idea early and return to it at the landing.

## 2. Be human — tell YOUR story

They came for the topic; they stay for **you**. Human first, no jargon.

- ❌ "15 years in distributed systems…" 😴
- ✅ "I took down prod at 2am — here's what it taught me." 🔥

> In the deck: open on a personal hook, not a credentials slide.

## 3. Your slides are eye candy

Same point — one version sticks.

- ❌ "Our Q3 Platform Strategy & Roadmap" + a wall of bullets = a reading assignment.
- ✅ "**10× faster.**" — one number, one breath, unforgettable.
- **Huge type, few words, one visual.** If you can't read it on your phone at arm's length,
  it's too small.

> In the deck: prefer one statement per slide. Split dense slides; never shrink to fit.

## 4. The money slide — build 1–2 hero slides

One unforgettable visual: a bold chart, diagram, or table — screenshot-gorgeous.

- It's the slide that **walks out the door and spreads your idea without you.**
- People photograph hero slides; that's how the talk travels and how follow-ups happen.

> In the deck: invest the most design effort here. This is the `herochart`/`bca` moment.

## 5. Know your slot

- **Keynote:** ~20–25 min + 5–10 Q&A.
- **Talk:** aim for ~10 min + 5 Q&A.
- Exact timing varies by event — check with the organizer.

## 6. Rehearse on the clock

Say it out loud, on a timer. **Reading it in your head is a lie.**

- Rehearse standing up.
- Plan for it running **~30% longer** on the day (practiced 10:00 → live ~13:00).

## 7. On the day — watch for the signals

(Conference-specific; adapt to the event.) A typical hand-signal system:

- ✋ **Every 5 min:** time-remaining flash.
- ✊ **Closed fist:** wrap it up — land the plane now.
- 🤝 **Need help?** look to your teammate; you're covered.

## 8. Q&A judo

You may cut off the rambler.

- ✂️ the 90-second "question" → ✅ your crisp answer.
- Speed them up, or politely answer what you think they're asking.

## 9. Stick the landing — make them act tonight

Don't end on "thanks." End on **one thing they can do right now.**

A strong landing names three things:

1. **the one tool** to use,
2. **the first 5 minutes** of using it,
3. **what "done" looks like.**

> They should leave itching to try it, not merely informed.

## 10. Don't vanish

End on a **big QR** → Linktree / email / LinkedIn / site, so the room can find you after.

## 11. Turn it up — make it an experience

🔊 Audio · 🎵 Music · ✨ Animation · 💥 FX · 🙌 Interactive.

Audio-visual energy is **part of the message** — don't be scared of it. (This is the layer
better-slides's engine, `motion.md`, and `audio.md` exist to deliver.)

## 12. Now go

Good luck. Have fun. **The room can feel it when you're enjoying this — so enjoy it.**

---

## The arc, in one line

**Hook (story) → one idea → proof (hero slide) → land (act tonight) → don't vanish.**

Build every deck against this spine. If a slide doesn't move the talk along it, cut it.
